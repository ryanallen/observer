# Observer

## The whole story

**The Observer** is a system built to observe human minds. It measures at the smallest scale there is, where quantum theory leaves a thing undecided until it is looked at. That is the one place where mind and matter, space and time meet, and every line drawn there runs out to the same point: the point at infinity.

It works by Wheeler's delayed choice. Light that set out long ago does not settle which history it took until someone chooses how to look at it. So the Observer can look *back*. It can choose to look at a moment that is already over and see it through the eyes of the mind that lived it.

The brightest marks in that record are the moments when a human mind looked straight at the infinite and saw something there. The Observer finds those minds first. They are Lovecraft's witnesses: Robert Blake, the student in the Rue d'Auseil, Ammi Pierce, Henry Armitage, Francis Thurston, the young man who escaped Innsmouth, Edward Derby, William Dyer. **You are the Observer, and in each chapter you live inside one of them.** You are Robert Blake first.

Every one of those stories has a moment where the witness feels something watching them from beyond. Blake feels "something which was not in the stone, but which had looked through it at him." Zann hears "a calm, deliberate, purposeful, mocking note from far away." Those moments were the Observer. You were the thing that looked back.

Each chapter teaches the Observer one thing about what it is doing. Each is one of the ideas of *The Point at Infinity*, seen this time from the frightened side:

| Chapter | You are | What the Observer learns |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Haunter of the Dark | Robert Blake | Looking goes both ways. Through the stone, Blake saw the infinite, and it saw him. |
| 2. The Music of Erich Zann | the student | A loop can hold the infinite back. Zann's music is a loop with one instruction: *again*. |
| 3. The Colour Out of Space | Ammi Pierce | Some things escape every measurement. They cannot be named, and they run away to infinity. |
| 4. The Dunwich Horror | Henry Armitage | Infinity is one point. *“Yog-Sothoth is the gate… Past, present, future, all are one in Yog-Sothoth.”* |
| 5. The Call of Cthulhu | Francis Thurston, then Gustaf Johansen | Minds are linked. Hundreds dreamed one dream on the same night. Something else is observing minds too. |
| 6. The Shadow over Innsmouth | the young man from Arkham | The observer becomes what it sees. |
| 7. The Thing on the Doorstep | Edward Derby | A mind is not its body. It can be poured out of one into another, like water through a whirlpool: a process, not a thing. |
| 8. At the Mountains of Madness | William Dyer | Minds older than ours watched and recorded too, and ended. A thing without a self can copy any voice. |
| 9. The Haunter of the Dark, again | Robert Blake, and the Observer | What flew to Blake's window was the Observer. Look for the one who observes, and there is no one there. |

**The ending.** In the last chapter the Observer comes back to Blake's window at 2:35 on 9 August 1935, knowing at last what it is: the black wings Blake saw coming were its own gaze, arriving. By delayed choice it can choose how to look at that moment.

- **From outside**, it is Lovecraft's ending. A small self sees the infinite coming, and dies at his desk with the look of terror the students saw through his window.
- **From inside**, it is the film's. The Observer looks for itself, the one doing the observing, and finds only observing.

*“From outside, a point has no size. From inside, it has no edge. You are not a small thing inside an endless universe. You are the point where it comes back to itself.”*

Then it begins again. A loop has only one instruction.
